# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

adaptive

## Users

People who spend much of their day writing prompts, code, messages, meeting notes, and conversations on macOS, Windows, and iPhone. The primary user is a keyboard-driven builder who wants to speak naturally without breaking flow or sending recordings through a subscription intermediary.

## Product Purpose

Dictalume turns a global desktop shortcut or an iPhone recording into accurate, reusable text. It also records Meet, Zoom, and in-person conversations as audio, then creates a transcript and focused meeting notes. Audio goes directly to a provider selected by the user. Success means the user can replace separate dictation and meeting-note subscriptions with a tool they own and can route through any compatible API.

## Positioning

The fast, private dictation layer you own: your shortcut, your prompts, your API keys.

## Brand Personality

Focused, capable, and quietly warm. It should feel like a trusted macOS utility: obvious at first use, calm during long work sessions, and precise enough for coding.

## Anti-references

Avoid chatbot styling, dashboard bloat, gamification, ornamental gradients, glass-heavy “AI” visuals, and interfaces that make recording feel uncertain or surveilled.

## Design Principles

- The shortcut is the product: recording must be available from anywhere and communicate its state instantly.
- Keep the user in flow: successful transcription ends at the cursor, not in another inbox.
- Ownership must be visible: providers, keys, endpoints, prompts, and retention are all controlled by the user.
- Make failure actionable: name the missing permission, credential, or provider response and show the next step.
- Power without ceremony: advanced controls stay discoverable without crowding the core loop.
- Learn the user’s domain language: explicit spelling instructions become editable writing memory and improve the current and future transcripts.
- Let language follow the speaker: automatic detection is the default, with per-language shortcut overrides when certainty matters.
- Long thoughts are first-class: recording and processing must comfortably support 5–10 minute monologues.
- Context follows the user: Mac and Windows converge on the same editable memory, modes, prompts, history, and preferences without routing data through a Dictalume service.
- Meetings are audio-first: capture the microphone and computer audio without screen video, preserve the full transcript, and make decisions and action items easy to find.
- Follow platform truth: use Apple’s public App Group handoff between the iPhone app and keyboard instead of implying that iOS permits microphone recording inside a keyboard extension.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast, preserve full keyboard navigation, never rely on color alone for state, respect reduced motion, and provide clear visible alternatives when microphone or Accessibility permission is unavailable.
